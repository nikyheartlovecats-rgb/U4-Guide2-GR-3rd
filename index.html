Unit 4: Grammar Theory Reference

Welcome to our digital whiteboard session! In Unit 4, we learn how to talk about past activities using Simple Past Wh- questions and negative sentences, as well as expression formulas like go + verb + -ing.

Part 1: Simple Past (Wh- Questions and Negatives)

We use the auxiliary verb did and didn't (did not) for past actions in questions and negatives. Notice that the main verb stays in its base form when using *did/didn't*!

Type Formula / Structure Example
Wh- Question Wh- word + did + subject + base verb + ...? What did you do last weekend?
Negative Subject + didn't + base verb + ... I didn't go out. I stayed home.
Yes/No Question Did + subject + base verb + ...? Did your team win? No, they didn't.

Part 2: Expressing Hobbies with "go + verb + -ing"

To talk about leisure activities and sports in the past, present, or future, we combine the verb go (or *goes*, *went*) with an activity ending in -ing.

Tense Subject Structure Example
Present I / You / We / They go + verb-ing I go swimming on weekends.
Present (3rd person) He / She / It goes + verb-ing She goes hiking in the mountains.
Past All subjects (I, He, She...) went + verb-ing They went shopping yesterday.
Past Negative All subjects didn't go + verb-ing He didn't go fishing last week.

Resolved Examples (15 Total: 5 Basic, 5 Intermediate, 5 Advanced)

Review these fully solved examples step-by-step to understand how grammar structures are applied correctly.

Basic Level Examples

Example B1: Negative Past Statement

Prompt: Complete with *didn't* + verb: "I ____ (visit) a museum. It was interesting."

Solution: I didn't visit a museum.
Explanation: Use "didn't" followed by the base verb "visit".

Example B2: Wh- Question in Past

Prompt: Form the question: "How / your weekend?"

Solution: How was your weekend?
Explanation: With the verb 'to be' in the past, we use "was" instead of "did". Answer: "It was exciting."

Example B3: Present 'go + -ing'

Prompt: Complete: "She ____ (go swimming) on Saturdays."

Solution: She goes swimming.
Explanation: For third-person singular (She), "go" changes to "goes" in present simple.

Example B4: Past 'go + -ing'

Prompt: Complete: "They ____ (go hiking) last Sunday."

Solution: They went hiking.
Explanation: In the past tense, "go" becomes the irregular past form "went".

Example B5: Simple Past Yes/No Question

Prompt: Answer the question: "Did you eat out?" (Negative answer)

Solution: No, I didn't. We ate pizza at home.
Explanation: Short negative answers require "didn't".
Intermediate Level Examples

Example I1: Wh- Question and Answer Pair

Prompt: Match: "What did you do last weekend?" with "I stayed home / fly."

Solution: I stayed home.
Explanation: The question asks about past actions, so the response uses past tense verbs ("stayed").

Example I2: Negative with team sports

Prompt: Fill in: "I played soccer, but my team ____ (win). I was sad."

Solution: my team didn't win.
Explanation: Since the person was sad, the team lost, expressed as the negative past "didn't win".

Example I3: Past Negative 'go + -ing'

Prompt: Complete: "He ____ (go shopping) yesterday; he stayed indoors."

Solution: He didn't go shopping.
Explanation: Past negative uses "didn't go" + verb-ing.

Example I4: Interrogative 'go + -ing'

Prompt: Form question: "What / you / do / on the weekend? (fishing)"

Solution: What did you do on the weekend? I went fishing.
Explanation: Combining past Wh- question structure with the "go + -ing" activity.

Example I5: Irregular Verbs in Negative Context

Prompt: Complete: "My brother ____ (lose) the game; he won first place!"

Solution: My brother didn't lose the game.
Explanation: Winning means he did not lose ("didn't lose").
Advanced Level Examples

Example A1: Multi-clause Past Contrast

Prompt: Complete: "I texted my friend, but he ____ (be busy) and ____ (text) me back."

Solution: ...he was busy and didn't text me back.
Explanation: Combines past state ("was") with past negative action ("didn't text").

Example A2: Complex Wh- Question Formulation

Prompt: Formulate a question for: "She went horseback riding through the forest."

Solution: What did she do last weekend? / Where did she go horseback riding?
Explanation: Uses auxiliary "did" with base verb "do" or "go".

Example A3: Comparative Past Activities

Prompt: Answer: "What didn't Mai do? (riding horses / fishing)" based on image where she rode a horse.

Solution: She didn't go fishing. She went horseback riding.
Explanation: Contrast between what was done (went + -ing) and what wasn't done (didn't go + -ing).

Example A4: Third-person vs First-person Past Negative

Prompt: Correct the error: "He didn't stayed home yesterday."

Solution: He didn't stay home yesterday.
Explanation: After "didn't", the verb must return to its base form ("stay", not "stayed").

Example A5: Integrated Storytelling

Prompt: Complete: "My family ____ (eat out). We ____ (stay) home and ____ (cook) dinner."

Solution: My family didn't eat out. We stayed home and cooked dinner.
Explanation: Combining a negative past statement with affirmative past regular verbs.

Critical Reflection Questions

Think deeply about these questions and discuss them with your classmates or teacher to build strong conceptual logic.

  • Why do we use the base form of the main verb (like *go* or *stay*) after the auxiliary word *didn't* in past negative sentences?

    Hint: Think about how the word "did" already shows that the sentence is in the past tense, so the main verb doesn't need to change again!

  • How does changing *go* to *went* or *goes* change the time or person in sentences with *go + -ing* (like *go swimming*)?

    Hint: Compare "She goes swimming every week" with "She went swimming last weekend." What do the words *goes* and *went* tell us about when the action happened?

  • Imagine you are interviewing a friend about their last vacation. Why are Wh- questions (using *What*, *Where*, *How*) more useful than Yes/No questions (*Did you...*)?

    Hint: Consider whether a Yes/No question gives you detailed stories or just short answers.

  • When talking about team sports or games, what is the difference between saying "My team won" and "My team didn't win"?

    Hint: Think about the outcome of the game and how negation shifts the meaning completely.

Question 1: Wh- Question Correction

Solution: What did you go last Saturday? (Or: Where did you go?)

Solution: I didn't visit the science museum.
Explanation: Change "visited" to "didn't visit" (base form).

Question 3: 'go + -ing' Past Application

Task: Complete: "Last summer, my sister and I ____ (go hiking) in the mountains."

Solution: Last summer, my sister and I went hiking in the mountains.
Explanation: "Last summer" indicates past tense, so "go" becomes "went".

Question 4: Dialogue Completion

Task: Complete the dialogue:
A: "____ you eat out on Friday?"
B: "No, we ____. We cooked dinner at home."

Solution: Did you eat out on Friday? No, we didn't.
Explanation: Past question starts with "Did" and short negative response uses "didn't".
